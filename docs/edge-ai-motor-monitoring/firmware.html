Motor-monitoring firmware, end to end

Three sensors and a motor feed seven FreeRTOS tasks. They condense 200 Hz samples into a 14-feature row every 15 ms, keep the last 30 rows as a sliding window, and every 10th row wake a 3-head neural network that decides whether the motor is healthy. The verdict goes out on the UART as CSV and to a browser dashboard over Ethernet.

The seven tasks, by priority

CMSIS-RTOS2 priorities map to FreeRTOS numbers (Low 8, BelowNormal 16, Normal 24, AboveNormal 32, High 40). Stack sizes are in bytes and come out of the 100 KB FreeRTOS heap. All seven together take 76 KB of it.

Also running: the FreeRTOS Idle task (priority 0) whenever nothing else is ready, and the timer service task (priority 2), which never wakes because no software timers are created. Interrupts that touch the RTOS (GPDMA1 channels 0–3, I2C1, SPI5, USART1, ETH) sit at NVIC priority 5, exactly at configMAX_SYSCALL_INTERRUPT_PRIORITY, so their callbacks may release semaphores. The HAL time base runs on TIM6 at priority 15; FreeRTOS uses SysTick.

How data crosses between tasks

Handshakes, locks and flags

There are no queues in this design. Tasks share global structs, guard them with short critical sections or mutexes, and signal readiness with flags and binary semaphores.

The 14-feature row

Built in the training's column order. The network subtracts the training mean and divides by the standard deviation itself (its first two layers), so the firmware passes raw values in. The firmware also keeps its own normalised copy, because the reconstruction error is measured in normalised units.

Inside the network, and how the verdict is made

The network is a pure MLP with two encoders that share the flattened 420-value input. One is an autoencoder that tries to rebuild its input; a poor rebuild means the window looks unlike training data. The other feeds a 7-class classifier and a 1-value anomaly logit. post_process() combines all three heads with fixed thresholds.
